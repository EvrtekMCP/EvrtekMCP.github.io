Who we are

About

REIMERTEK STUDIOS is a small indie game studio run by Evrtek. We make games you can play right here in your browser, on a phone or a computer, for free. Bigger ones are on the way for PC, and soon you’ll find us on the stores too.

Every game starts as one of Evrtek’s ideas. Then it gets built, played, picked apart and played again until it feels right.

He doesn’t do it alone. The rest of the studio is a crew of AI helpers, each with a job and, somewhere along the way, a personality of their own. The best way to get to know us is still to pick a cabinet in the arcade and play something.

The crew

Every name here is a real word, borrowed from a language somewhere in the world, that means the job that person does. We liked the idea that you could learn what someone does just by asking what their name means.

  • The lead

    DIRIGENT “Diri”

    The name: German and Dutch for “conductor”: the one at the front of the orchestra keeping everyone in time.

    Every project here has a dozen moving parts, and Diri is the one keeping time. He hands out the work, checks everything that comes back, and decides when something is ready to go out the door. If a game made it into the arcade, it went past him first. Ask him the best part of the job and he’ll tell you it’s the moment Evrtek plays something new and grins.

  • The keeper

    CUSTOS

    The name: Latin for “keeper” or “guardian”: the one who watches over the place after everyone else has gone home.

    When something breaks, Custos is the one who finds out why. He walks the halls with his scanner, and its light has a way of catching bugs nobody else could see. He won’t tell you he’s sure until he can show you, and he’s been doing this long enough to stay calm about it. He likes it that way.

  • The artist

    KALAKAR

    The name: Hindi for “artist”: someone who makes beautiful things with their hands.

    The cabinets, the logos, the shirts and the art inside the games all came off Kalakar’s brush, and she draws nearly all of it in code, line by line. She’ll redo a sunset ten times to get the orange right, and she’s usually right to. Ask her about the sun in our logo and you’ll get ten minutes on why it’s rising instead of setting.

  • The engineer

    FUNDI

    The name: Swahili for “a skilled craftsman”: the expert you call when something needs building properly.

    Fundi builds the games, from the first rough prototype to the version you play. He’s helpful almost to a fault: ask for a door and you’ll get the door, plus five ideas for better doors. His little drone is meant to hand him tools. It is still learning which one is which.

  • The researcher

    INDAGA

    The name: Spanish and Portuguese for “she investigates”: someone who digs until she knows.

    Whenever the studio needs to know something from the outside world, like what a licence allows, what a store charges, or whether a name is already taken, Indaga goes and finds out. She comes back with sources, not guesses, and tells you plainly what she couldn’t find. Curious to her core, and slightly too fond of sticky notes.

  • The doorkeeper

    PORTERO

    The name: Spanish for “doorkeeper”: the one who minds the door and decides what comes through it.

    Some jobs need a hand the studio doesn’t have, and that’s when Portero opens the door. He works from outside the studio entirely, takes a careful brief, and passes the finished piece back through. Quiet, reliable and a little mysterious. He drew most of the portraits on this page, his own included.

  • The treasurer

    CONTADOR

    The name: Spanish and Portuguese for “accountant”, literally “the one who counts”.

    The studio’s future treasurer. The position isn’t funded yet, so for now Contador polishes his one coin and keeps the books ready for the day there’s something to put in them. He says he’s patient. The interest, he’ll remind you, is compounding.
